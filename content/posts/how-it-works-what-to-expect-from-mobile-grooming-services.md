Mobile dog grooming is simple once you have done it, but the first time raises a lot of small questions. Do I need to be home? Where does the van park? When do I pay? This post walks through a Pet Rinse Repeat mobile appointment from booking to rebooking so you know exactly what is coming.

If you are still deciding between the van and the salon, start with our [mobile vs. salon comparison](/blog/mobile-vs-salon-dog-grooming-kansas-city/). If you have already decided on mobile, read on.

## Step 1: Check your service area

The van covers the Kansas City metro on both sides of the state line. Before anything else, find your city on the [service areas](/service-areas/) page. Each city has its own page with details for that area.

Two exceptions to know up front. Downtown Kansas City is not serviced by the van because of parking restrictions, so downtown clients book the [salon](/salon-grooming/) in North Kansas City instead. And giant deshed breeds (Bernese Mountain Dogs, Great Pyrenees, Newfoundlands, St. Bernards) are salon-only.

## Step 2: Book your time slot online

Mobile booking is direct scheduling, not a request form. You go to [mobile grooming](/mobile-grooming/), choose the service, and pick an open date and time. That slot is your appointment.

Mobile appointments typically book one to three weeks out depending on your area, so if your dog's coat is getting long, book sooner rather than later.

When you book, you will provide the information the groomer needs to plan your dog's visit:

- Your name, phone, email, and address
- Your dog's name, breed or mix, and weight
- Coat type and the length or style you want
- When your dog was last groomed and how the coat is now (any mats or tangles)
- Behavior notes: how your dog handles handling, nails, dryers, strangers, other dogs

That last one matters more than people expect. Groomers can work with nearly any temperament when they know what is coming. An undisclosed biting or reactive history carries a $300 fee, but the real reason to be upfront is that it lets the groomer keep your dog, and themselves, safe.

A few eligibility points for the van: dogs over 20 pounds need to walk to and from the van and stand on their own during the groom, and standard and giant doodles are groomed in the van at a summer-cut length or shorter. If you want a longer doodle cut, book the salon.

## Step 3: Before the van arrives

On appointment day you will have a three-hour arrival window. Within that window, the groomer will let you know when they are heading your way.

Here is how to get ready:

- **Parking.** The van needs a flat, accessible driveway or street parking. If your street is tight or your driveway slopes, mention it in your booking notes so the groomer can plan.
- **Potty break.** A walk or yard break shortly before the window starts makes for a more comfortable groom.
- **Light meal.** Skip a big meal right before the appointment. A full stomach and a bath are not a great combination for some dogs.
- **Collar and accessories.** Take off bandanas, bows, and anything that is not a basic collar or harness.
- **Clear the path.** Make sure there is an easy route from your door to the curb or driveway.
- **Other pets.** Keep other dogs and cats inside and away from the hand-off. Your dog should get the groomer's full attention from the first minute.
- **Be reachable.** Someone needs to hand your dog off and receive them afterward, so keep your phone close during the window.

## Step 4: Meeting the groomer

When the van arrives, the groomer will come to your door, meet your dog, and go over the plan: the cut you asked for, anything they notice about the coat, and any adjustments they recommend. If your dog's coat has mats or tangles, this is where the groomer will talk through what is realistic. The groomer sets the plan based on what the coat can safely handle.

Ask questions here. Bring up anything that worries you about your dog's behavior, past grooming experiences, or sensitive spots. The groomer would much rather know now.

Then your dog walks (or, for small dogs, is carried) to the van.

## Step 5: The groom itself

Everything happens inside the van, one dog at a time. There is no other dog waiting, no kennel, and no unfamiliar building. For many dogs, especially nervous ones, this is the single biggest advantage of mobile grooming.

A full-service groom includes a breed-appropriate haircut, bath and blow dry, de-shedding as needed, ear cleaning, and a sanitary trim. A nail trim is an add-on you can select when you book or ask about at hand-off.

Timing varies with size, coat, and temperament. Most dogs are done in one to two hours; a large, curly, or overdue coat takes longer. Because PRR bills by hands-on grooming time with a one-hour minimum and 15-minute increments after that, the time your dog actually needs is what you pay for. Our [grooming cost guide](/blog/dog-grooming-cost-kansas-city/) explains the math.

You do not need to hover. Many owners go back to whatever they were doing and wait for the knock.

## Step 6: Hand-off, payment, and aftercare

When the groom is finished, the groomer brings your dog back to your door and tells you how it went: how your dog handled it, what they noticed about the coat, skin, ears, or nails, and anything worth keeping an eye on. This is a groomer's observation, not a medical opinion; if something sounds concerning, your vet is the right next call.

Payment is simple. Mobile grooming is credit card only, kept on file from booking, and there is no cash for services. Cash tips are welcome if you want to leave one. The total is the hourly service time plus the $20 travel fee plus any add-ons.

Two policies to keep in mind. Cancellations within 24 hours are charged 50%; same-day cancellations and no-shows are charged 100%. Life happens, so if you need to move an appointment, do it as early as you can.

## Step 7: Rebooking

Mobile clients stay on a recurring 4-, 6-, or 8-week schedule. The groomer will recommend an interval based on your dog's coat and the length you like to keep it. Shorter cuts and well-brushed coats can usually stretch longer; longer styles and coats that mat easily need the shorter gap.

A standing schedule does two things. It keeps the coat in a condition where each groom is quicker (and therefore less expensive), and it keeps your spot on the route in your area, which matters when appointments are booking weeks out.

## Quick answers

**Do I need to be home?** Someone needs to be there to hand your dog off and receive them at the end. Ask at booking if your situation is unusual.

**What if it rains?** The groom happens inside the van, so weather is rarely an issue beyond the walk to and from the door.

**Can you do two dogs?** Yes. Each dog is groomed one at a time, and the travel fee is per visit. Nail trim and dremel for an additional pet is $20.

**My dog has never been groomed.** Puppy's first groom is available in the van at $130/hr ($140 for oodles). Say so in your booking notes so the groomer can plan a gentle pace.

**My dog is anxious or has bitten before.** Tell us at booking. Disclosed behavior is something the groomer can plan for.
