Pet Rinse Repeat runs both a mobile grooming van and a salon in North Kansas City, and we get asked which one is "better" more than almost anything else. The honest answer is that they are good at different things. The right choice depends on your dog, your schedule, where you live, and what you want the appointment to feel like.

This post lays out how each option actually works, puts them side by side, and gives you a short set of questions to decide for your own dog. Prices referenced here are from our [pricing page](/pricing/), and a full breakdown is in our [grooming cost guide](/blog/dog-grooming-cost-kansas-city/).

## What a mobile grooming appointment involves

With mobile grooming, a fully equipped van parks at your home and your dog is groomed inside it, one-on-one, start to finish. No car ride, no waiting area, no other dogs.

A few practical details that shape whether mobile fits you:

- **Scheduling.** You pick a real time slot online. Mobile appointments typically book one to three weeks out depending on your area, and you get a three-hour arrival window on the day. The van runs Monday through Sunday, 8:30 AM to 5:30 PM, across the Kansas City metro on both sides of the state line. Check your city on our [service areas](/service-areas/) page.
- **Recurring schedule.** Mobile clients stay on a 4-, 6-, or 8-week rotation, which keeps the coat manageable and keeps your spot on the route.
- **Parking.** The van needs a flat, accessible driveway or street parking. Downtown Kansas City is not serviced by the van because of parking restrictions; downtown clients are welcome at the salon.
- **Your dog's part.** Dogs over 20 pounds need to walk to and from the van and stand on their own during the groom.
- **Breed limits.** Giant deshed breeds (Bernese Mountain Dog, Great Pyrenees, Newfoundland, St. Bernard) are salon-only. Standard and giant doodles are groomed in the van at a summer-cut length or shorter.
- **Payment.** Credit card only, kept on file. Cash tips are welcome.
- **Price.** Mobile haircuts and desheds start at $130/hr ($140 for oodles) with a one-hour minimum, plus a $20 travel fee per visit.

If you want the full walkthrough, read [what to expect at your first mobile appointment](/blog/how-it-works-what-to-expect-from-mobile-grooming-services/).

## What a salon grooming appointment involves

The Pet Rinse Repeat Groomery & Boutique is at 2030 Swift Street in North Kansas City. You book a time slot online, bring your dog in, and the groom happens in the salon.

- **Hours.** Tuesday through Thursday 8:30 AM to 7:00 PM; Friday through Monday 8:30 AM to 5:30 PM. The later weekday evenings are handy if you work a standard day.
- **No geography limits.** If you can drive to North Kansas City, you can book. That makes the salon the answer for downtown residents and for anyone outside the van's route.
- **Every size and coat.** The salon handles the giant deshed breeds the van cannot, and doodles can be left longer than a summer cut.
- **Puppy options.** The salon offers a puppy's first groom at a flat $100 and a 2-week puppy desensitizing visit with nail trim for $40, a gentle way to get a young dog used to the process.
- **À la carte services.** Nail care ($20), ear cleaning ($15), sanitary trim ($25), paw pads ($20), and more can be booked without a full groom.
- **Price.** Salon haircuts and desheds start at $100/hr ($120 for oodles), one-hour minimum, no travel fee. Bath and brush for flat-coated dogs runs $60 to $125 by weight.
- **Cats too.** The salon grooms cats as well, and our [Here Kitty](/cat-grooming/) van handles cat-only mobile grooming.

## Questions to ask when choosing for your dog

**How does your dog handle the car and new places?** Many dogs are fine. Some get carsick, pant the whole way, or shut down in a new building. For those dogs, a groom in the driveway removes the hardest part of the day before it starts.

**How does your dog do around other dogs?** The van is a one-dog space. If your dog is reactive or simply overstimulated by other animals, that matters. Please disclose any biting or reactive history when you book; undisclosed biting or reactive history carries a $300 fee, and more importantly, telling us lets the groomer plan a safe appointment.

**Can your dog manage the physical part?** Mobile grooming asks dogs over 20 pounds to walk to the van and stand through the groom. Some seniors or dogs with mobility issues are better served in the salon.

**What breed and cut are you after?** Giant deshed breeds go to the salon. If you want a long, fluffy doodle cut, that is a salon appointment too.

**Where do you live?** Downtown residents book the salon. If you are elsewhere in the metro, look up your city on [service areas](/service-areas/).

**How much is your time worth on grooming day?** A salon visit means two drives and working around drop-off. Mobile means being home for a three-hour window. Different households find different things easier.

**What is your budget?** For a standard full groom, mobile runs $30 more per hour plus the travel fee. Over a year of 6-week appointments that adds up, and it is a fair trade for some families and not for others. Neither answer is wrong.

## How PRR helps you choose

You do not have to get this perfect on the first try. Both booking flows ask for your dog's breed, size, coat, and behavior, and the groomer uses that to plan the appointment. If something about your dog suggests the other setting would be a better fit, the groomer will say so, and switching is just a matter of booking the other service next time.

A few owners also use both: salon for the big deshed on the Husky, mobile for the small dog who hates the car. There is no rule that one household picks one service.

What we will not tell you is that either setting is right for every dog. The van is not set up for giant breeds or dogs who cannot stand through a groom, and the salon is not the calmest option for a dog who melts down around other animals. Matching the dog to the setting is the whole point.
