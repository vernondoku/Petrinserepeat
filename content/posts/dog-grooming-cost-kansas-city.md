If you have looked for dog grooming prices in Kansas City, you have probably noticed that nobody gives a straight number. That is not groomers being cagey. A ten-pound Maltese with a well-kept coat and a ninety-pound Goldendoodle who skipped a few brushings are very different jobs, and pricing them the same would be unfair to one of them.

This post explains what actually drives a grooming price, how Pet Rinse Repeat's hourly structure works for both mobile and salon appointments, and how to come up with a realistic estimate for your own dog before you book. Current rates live on our [pricing page](/pricing/); the numbers below are pulled from it as of the date on this post.

## Why grooming prices vary from dog to dog

Most professional groomers, whether they charge by the hour or by a flat menu, are really pricing the same handful of things:

- **The service you want.** A bath and brush takes much less time than a full haircut. A deshed on a double-coated breed is its own kind of work.
- **Size.** Bigger dog, more coat, more time to wash, dry, and cut.
- **Coat type.** Curly and wavy coats (poodles and doodles especially) take longer to dry and scissor than a short flat coat.
- **Coat condition.** A coat that has been brushed at home comes apart easily. A coat with tangles or mats has to be worked through carefully, and that adds time.
- **Temperament.** A dog who stands calmly gets done faster than one who needs frequent breaks. No judgment, just time.
- **Where the groom happens.** A van that drives to you costs more to run than a fixed salon, so mobile rates generally run higher.

Flat-price menus hide these differences inside a range. Hourly pricing puts them in the open: your dog costs what your dog takes.

## How PRR prices a groom

Pet Rinse Repeat uses the same structure in the van and in the salon:

- **Haircuts and desheds are billed by the hour** with a **one-hour minimum**. After the first hour, time is billed in **15-minute increments**, each a quarter of the hourly rate.
- **You are charged for hands-on grooming time**, not the total time your dog is with us. Drying while the groomer preps the next step, a water break, or a dog who needs a minute to settle does not run up the clock the way it would if we billed wall-clock time.
- **Bath and brush services for flat-coated dogs are flat-rate by weight**, because the time is predictable enough to price that way.
- **Mobile adds a $20 travel fee per visit.**

A full-service groom includes a breed-appropriate haircut, bath and blow dry, de-shedding as needed, ear cleaning, and a sanitary trim. A nail trim is an add-on.

## Mobile vs. salon: paying for convenience

The mobile hourly rate is $30 higher than the salon rate for a standard full-service groom, plus the $20 travel fee. What you get for that is a groomer who comes to your driveway, works one-on-one with your dog in the van, and hands your dog back without a car ride or a waiting area.

For some dogs and some households, that is clearly worth it. For others, a salon appointment at the lower rate makes more sense, especially for dogs that do fine in a car and owners who are near North Kansas City anyway. A few situations route to the salon regardless: giant deshed breeds like Bernese Mountain Dogs, Great Pyrenees, Newfoundlands, and St. Bernards are salon-only, and the van does not service downtown Kansas City because of parking restrictions.

## What can change the estimate

Because the price is built on time, anything that changes the time changes the price. The common ones:

- **Coat condition.** Tangles and matting are the biggest variable. Regular brushing at home between appointments is the most reliable way to keep grooming time (and cost) down.
- **Rate category.** For desheds, the groomer sets the size category based on your dog's coat and build, not just the number on the scale.
- **Add-ons.** A nail trim or extra scissoring adds to the total.
- **Dogs who need extra time.** Puppies, seniors, and nervous dogs sometimes need a slower pace. That is the right call for the dog, and it is billed as hands-on time like everything else.

The groomer will tell you if a groom is running meaningfully longer than expected. You will not get a surprise at checkout.

Plan for the cancellation policy too: cancellations within 24 hours are charged 50%, and same-day cancellations and no-shows are charged 100%.

## How to estimate your own dog's groom

You do not need to call for a quote. The hourly structure lets you ballpark it yourself:

1. Pick the service (full groom, deshed, bath and brush, or tidy).
2. Pick mobile or salon.
3. Estimate the time. A small dog with a tidy coat in good shape often lands near the one-hour minimum. A large or curly-coated dog, or a coat that has gone a while between grooms, will run longer.
4. Add the $20 travel fee for mobile, plus any add-ons.

For illustration only, not a quote: a medium doodle in the van who takes an hour and a half at $140/hr comes to $210 plus the $20 travel fee, or $230. The same dog in the salon at $120/hr for the same time would be $180. Your dog's actual time will be whatever your dog's actual time is.

### What helps the groomer give you a realistic picture

When you book online you will enter your dog's details. The more honest and specific you are, the closer the first appointment lands to what you expected:

- Breed or mix, and weight
- Coat type and the length you want
- When your dog was last groomed and how the coat is doing now (mats, tangles, shedding)
- Any behavior to be aware of, including past biting or reactivity. Please disclose this up front; an undisclosed biting or reactive history carries a $300 fee.
