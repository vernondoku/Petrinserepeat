Bringing a puppy to the groomer for the first time is a bigger deal than it looks. The haircut is the easy part. What actually matters is whether your puppy walks out thinking "that was fine" or "never again," because that first impression tends to stick for years.

This guide covers how Pet Rinse Repeat approaches a first puppy visit, what you should tell us before you book, and a few simple things you can do at home so the appointment goes smoothly. One thing we won't do is give you a universal starting age or a vaccine checklist. Those depend on your puppy and your vet, and we'd rather you ask us directly than read something here that doesn't fit your situation.

## Start with a conversation (or a note)

Before you book, it helps to know a few things about your puppy:

- **Age and breed or mix.** A 12-week-old Maltese and a 6-month-old doodle are very different first appointments.
- **Coat type.** Short, smooth coats usually just need a bath and a nail trim. Hair that keeps growing (poodles, doodles, Shih Tzus, Yorkies, and similar) needs to get used to clippers and scissors eventually.
- **Whether anyone has groomed them yet.** A puppy that's had a bath at home is in a different spot than one who's never heard a dryer.
- **How they handle being touched.** Paws, ears, mouth, tail. Be honest. No one is judging a puppy for being a puppy.
- **Vaccination status.** We don't list specific requirements in this post because that's something to confirm with us and with your vet. Ask when you book.

## An introduction is not a haircut

This is the part most people don't expect. A puppy's first visit is mostly about learning, not styling.

At the salon, PRR offers two puppy-specific options:

| Salon option | Price | What it's for |
|---|---|---|
| Puppy's first groom | $100 flat | A full first grooming appointment paced for a puppy |
| Puppy 2-week desensitizing + nail trim | $40 flat | A short, handling-focused visit with a nail trim, meant to build comfort between full grooms |

The first groom is where your puppy learns the whole routine: the tub, the dryer, the table, the clippers. The groomer works at your puppy's pace, and the finished haircut may be simpler than what you'd ask for on an adult dog. That's on purpose. A tidy face and feet on a relaxed puppy is a better outcome than a perfect breed trim on one who's been pushed past their limit.

The desensitizing visit is the follow-up. It's short, it's inexpensive, and it keeps the salon from being a scary once-every-two-months surprise. Puppies who come in for a quick nail trim and some handling in between full grooms tend to settle into the routine faster.

## Gentle preparation at home

You don't need a training program. You need a few minutes a day, a calm voice, and some treats. Everything below should be easy and short. If your puppy gets squirmy, stop and try again tomorrow.

**Touch everything, briefly.** Handle each paw for a second or two, including between the toes. Lift an ear flap. Look at their teeth. Run your hand down the tail. Pair it with a treat and move on. You're teaching them that hands in odd places are boring, not alarming.

**Introduce the brush early.** Even a short-coated puppy benefits from a soft brush a few times a week. For coats that will need regular haircuts, this is the single most useful habit you can build. Puppies who are used to being brushed are far easier to groom as adults, and their coats stay in better shape between appointments.

**Make noise normal.** A hair dryer on low, held well away from the puppy and pointed at the floor, for a few seconds at a time. Treats during and after. Over a week or two, bring it a little closer. You're not drying the dog; you're just making the sound familiar.

**Practice standing still.** On a non-slip surface like a bath mat, ask your puppy to stand for a few seconds while you touch their back and sides. Grooming tables are a strange experience for a puppy, and standing calmly for short stretches at home takes some of the strangeness out of it.

**Skip the big bath.** You don't need to bathe your puppy right before the appointment. A clean coat helps, but a stressful tub experience the night before doesn't.

**Day of:** a light meal, a potty break, and a short walk so they arrive a little tired rather than wired. Bring your vet's contact information and any vaccine paperwork we've asked for.

## What to expect at the appointment

The groomer will meet your puppy before any tools come out, and will tell you what they're planning to do based on how the puppy is responding. Every full-service groom at PRR includes a breed-appropriate haircut, bath and blow dry, de-shedding as needed, ear cleaning, and a sanitary trim. Nail trimming is an add-on.

With puppies, "breed-appropriate" is often "shorter and simpler than the breed standard." If your puppy only tolerates a bath, a face tidy, and a nail trim on day one, that's still a successful first visit. The goal is a puppy who comes back.

After the appointment, the groomer can talk with you about what went well, what your puppy found hard, and what to work on at home before next time.

## Building the routine

A puppy who starts grooming young and comes back on a predictable schedule usually turns into an adult dog who is easy to groom. That's good for the dog, good for the groomer, and it tends to keep your costs down over time, since relaxed dogs with maintained coats take less hands-on time.

What the right schedule looks like depends on your puppy's coat and how much brushing you do at home. Your groomer can recommend an interval after the first visit. Many salon clients with growing-coat breeds alternate full grooms with the shorter desensitizing visit in the early months, then settle into a regular rhythm.
