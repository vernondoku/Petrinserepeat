A lot of people book "a groom" without knowing there are really three different services under that word: a bath & brush, a deshed, and a haircut. Pick the wrong one and you either pay for things your dog doesn't need or walk out with a dog that's still leaving fur on the couch.

This post explains what each service is for, how PRR's groomers decide which one fits, and what you can realistically expect afterward. No miracle claims. Shedding is what fur does; the goal is to manage it, not erase it.

## The three services, plainly

**Bath & brush**
A bath, blow dry, and brush-out. This is the right fit for smooth and short-coated dogs that shed lightly and don't need their hair cut: Boxers, Pit-type dogs, Boston Terriers, Dachshunds, and similar. At PRR it's flat-rate by weight rather than hourly, because the time is predictable.

**Deshedding**
For double-coated dogs that shed heavily: Huskies, Golden Retrievers, German Shepherds, Labs, Australian Shepherds, Pomeranians, Corgis. A deshed appointment is built around getting loose undercoat out. That typically means a bath with products that help loosen dead undercoat, a thorough high-velocity blow-out to push it free, and hands-on raking or carding to pull the rest. These dogs don't get a haircut; their coat is doing a job and should stay intact.

**Haircut (full-service groom)**
For dogs whose hair grows continuously and needs cutting: Poodles, doodles, Shih Tzus, Schnauzers, Yorkies, Maltese, Bichons, and many terriers. At PRR a full-service groom includes a breed-appropriate haircut, bath and blow dry, deshedding as needed, ear cleaning, and a sanitary trim. Nail trims are an add-on.

Here's the quick version:

| Your dog | Service | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Short, smooth coat; light shedding | Bath & brush | Needs cleaning, not cutting or heavy undercoat removal |
| Thick double coat; heavy shedding | Deshed | Loose undercoat is the problem; the coat itself should stay |
| Hair that keeps growing; gets tangled | Haircut | The coat needs to be shortened and shaped |

## Why you shouldn't shave a double coat

Owners of Huskies, Goldens, and Shepherds sometimes ask for a haircut to "stop the shedding." Most groomers will steer you away from that, and here's why.

A double coat has two layers: a soft, insulating undercoat and a coarser outer coat of guard hairs. The undercoat is what sheds in those spring and fall "coat blows." The guard hairs protect the skin from sun and help regulate temperature in both heat and cold. Shaving removes that protection and often changes how the coat grows back, sometimes permanently. Many groomers have seen double coats come back patchy, fuzzy, or uneven after a shave.

Shaving also doesn't actually fix shedding. The dog sheds the same amount of undercoat; the pieces are just shorter. A proper deshed removes the loose undercoat that's ready to fall out, which is what you're after.

## What the groomer needs to know

To recommend the right service and quote a realistic time, your groomer wants to know:

- **Breed or mix.** Even a best guess helps. "Shepherd-something, 65 pounds" is useful.
- **Weight.** Bath & brush is priced by weight, and it helps the groomer plan for desheds too.
- **Coat description.** Thick and fluffy, short and dense, long and silky, curly? Does it shed in clumps or just a steady trickle?
- **Current condition.** Blowing coat right now? Any mats or tangles? Been swimming or rolling in the yard?
- **What's bothering you.** Hair on the furniture, a dull coat, a doggy smell, or just a long-overdue cleanup. Different problems point to different services.

You can put this in the notes when you book online, or share it with the groomer at the appointment.

## How coat type and condition shape the choice

A couple of things people are often surprised by:

**Size category is set by the coat, not just the scale.** For desheds, PRR's groomer sets the size category based on coat and build. A 45-pound Australian Shepherd with a dense, long double coat is a lot more work than a 45-pound dog with a short coat, and the pricing reflects that. The two categories are small/medium double coat and large/XL double coat.

**Heavy shedders should book a deshed, not a full-service groom.** A full-service groom includes "deshedding as needed," which covers the light undercoat work that happens naturally during a haircut appointment. A Golden in full coat blow needs the dedicated deshed service.

**Short-coated dogs can shed a lot too.** Labs and Beagles are technically double-coated and can leave plenty of hair around. Depending on how heavy the shedding is, the groomer may recommend a deshed rather than a bath & brush. Ask when you book if you're not sure.

**Condition matters.** A double coat that's gone a long time without brushing can develop packed, felted undercoat that takes real time to work through. Haircuts and desheds at PRR are priced on hands-on grooming time, so a coat that arrives in rough shape costs more than one that's been brushed regularly.

## What to realistically expect after a deshed

This is where honesty matters. A good deshed removes a lot of loose undercoat, and many owners notice less hair around the house for a while afterward. What it doesn't do is stop a dog from shedding. Your Husky will still be a Husky.

A few realistic expectations:

- **Results fade as the next cycle of undercoat loosens.** During spring and fall coat blows, that can happen faster.
- **Regular appointments matter more than one big one.** Dogs on a 4-, 6-, or 8-week recurring schedule stay more manageable than dogs that come in once a year when the shedding gets desperate.
- **Home brushing extends the results.** An undercoat rake or deshedding tool once or twice a week, more during seasonal sheds, keeps the loose coat moving out between visits. Brush before baths, not after, and dry the coat fully.

If anyone promises you a specific percentage reduction in shedding, treat it as marketing, not grooming. Coats and dogs vary too much for that kind of number to mean anything.
